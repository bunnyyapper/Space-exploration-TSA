The temperature on the moon varies wildly depending on whether you are in a spot where the sun can reach, or in a dark area. Temperatures can be as high as 253 degrees Fahrenheit during the day, which is hotter than boiling water, and as low as -243 degrees Fahrenheit at night. In places that never see light, such as the bottom of some craters, the temperature can drop as low as -413 degrees Fahrenheit. That’s a temperature range of about 660 degrees! The hottest temperature on Earth ever recorded was 136 degrees Fahrenheit in the Libyan desert, and the coldest was -126 degrees Fahrenheit at the Vostok Station in Antarctica. That’s a range of 262 degrees, three times less than the temperature range on the moon!
